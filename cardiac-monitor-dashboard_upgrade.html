Why this reading

  • Chest pressure and its severity weigh the most — it's the strongest single signal.
  • Radiating pain, breathlessness, sweating, nausea and dizziness each add smaller weighted amounts.
  • Background risk factors (next tab) add a flat amount, since they shift baseline risk rather than signal an event happening now.
  • Score bands: 0–24 low · 25–49 moderate · 50–74 high · 75–100 emergency.

Know the signs

Common in most people

  • Pressure, squeezing or fullness in the center of the chest, lasting minutes or coming and going.
  • Pain spreading to arms, back, neck, jaw or stomach.
  • Shortness of breath, with or without chest discomfort.
  • Cold sweat, nausea, or lightheadedness.

Often under-recognized in women

  • Unusual fatigue, sometimes days beforehand.
  • Jaw, neck, back or upper-abdomen pain without obvious chest pain.
  • Breathlessness, nausea or vomiting as the leading symptom.
  • Lightheadedness or cold sweat with no clear chest pressure.

If this were real

  1. Call your local emergency number right away — don't drive yourself.
  2. Sit or lie down and loosen tight clothing while you wait.
  3. Chew an aspirin only if emergency services advise it and you're not allergic.
  4. If the person stops responding and isn't breathing normally, start CPR and use an AED if available.
  5. Note the time symptoms started — responders will ask, and it affects treatment.
